title: "Frequently Asked Questions About Heating Repair"

items:
  - question: "What are the signs that my heating system may need repair?"
    answer: "Common warning signs include uneven temperatures, weak or cool airflow, unusual noises, frequent cycling, difficulty reaching the thermostat setting, or a system that seems to run longer than normal. A complete loss of heat is an obvious problem, but smaller changes in performance can also indicate that the system should be evaluated."
  - question: "Why is my heating system running but my home still feels cold?"
    answer: "There are several possible reasons a heating system can operate without warming the home effectively. The issue may involve airflow, system controls, a component that is not functioning correctly, or another condition affecting performance. A proper inspection helps determine why the system is running without producing the level of comfort you expect."
  - question: "Should I repair my heating system or replace it?"
    answer: "The right decision depends on factors such as the condition and age of the equipment, the type of problem, how often repairs have been needed, and the overall performance of the system. A repair may make sense when the problem is isolated and the equipment is otherwise in reasonable condition, while recurring problems or declining performance may justify discussing replacement options."
  - question: "How long does a heating repair usually take?"
    answer: "Repair time depends on what is causing the problem and what work is required to correct it. Some issues can be addressed during a single service visit, while more involved problems may require additional time or a follow-up. The system should first be evaluated so the necessary repair can be identified."
  - question: "What should I do if my heating system suddenly stops working?"
    answer: "Start by checking that the thermostat is set correctly and that the heating system has power. If those basic checks do not restore heat, the system may need professional diagnosis. If you smell gas, a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or you believe there may be an immediate safety issue, leave the affected area and contact the appropriate emergency or utility service rather than attempting to troubleshoot the equipment yourself."
