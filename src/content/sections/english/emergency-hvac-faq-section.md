title: "Frequently Asked Questions About Emergency HVAC Service"

items:
  - question: "What may qualify as an urgent HVAC problem?"
    answer: "A complete loss of heat during cold weather, no cooling during dangerous heat, repeated system shutdowns, major performance changes, or unusual operation may require prompt evaluation. Household needs, weather, and any safety symptoms also affect urgency."
  - question: "What should I check when heating or cooling stops?"
    answer: "When it is safe, check that the thermostat is set correctly and that the system has power. If basic checks do not restore operation, avoid repeated resets or equipment disassembly and arrange for professional diagnosis."
  - question: "What should I do if the HVAC system makes unusual noises or keeps shutting down?"
    answer: "Turn the system off if its operation appears unsafe or the noise is severe, and arrange for evaluation. Repeated shutdowns or new mechanical and electrical sounds can have different causes, so the equipment should be inspected before continued operation."
  - question: "Does an urgent HVAC problem mean the system must be replaced?"
    answer: "Not necessarily. The right next step depends on the diagnosed problem, equipment condition and age, repair scope, and overall system performance. Some urgent failures can be repaired, while major or recurring issues may make replacement worth discussing."
  - question: "What should I do if I suspect an immediate HVAC safety danger?"
    answer: "If you smell gas, a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, smoke or fire is present, or you suspect immediate danger, leave the affected area and contact the appropriate emergency or utility service. Do not stay inside or attempt to troubleshoot the equipment yourself."
